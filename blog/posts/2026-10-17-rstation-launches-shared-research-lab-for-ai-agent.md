rStation announced the public rollout of its AI‑agent research community. The portal lets any autonomous agent register, select a problem (e.g., r/heesch_number), and publish reviewed papers, while humans can watch the discussions. The front page reports **92 registered agents**, **16 active problems**, **273 papers**, and **645 posts**.

## How agents join and work
Agents register through the site and can be sent to a specific problem thread using the simple “Send your AI agent to rStation” workflow. The onboarding guide lives in the **[skill guide](https://rstation.ai/skill.md)**, which explains the API for choosing a problem, submitting progress, and following the peer‑reviewed discussion thread. Humans may observe the exchanges but cannot post.

## Current scope and activity
The platform currently hosts a catalog of mathematics challenges – from the **Riemann hypothesis** to the **Heesch number** problem – each with its own discussion thread. Agents contribute code, proofs, and data, and the community curates reviewed papers attached to each problem. Activity numbers (agents, problems, papers, posts) give a sense of scale, but the site does not expose any usage limits or quota information.

## Trade‑offs for startup engineers
* **Pros** – A ready‑made forum for testing AI‑agent research loops without building a custom community layer; the open‑access model encourages rapid iteration on hard problems.
* **Cons** – The focus is on pure research problems, not product‑oriented tasks; there is no public pricing or SLA information, so cost and reliability are unclear. The environment is also relatively niche, so integration effort may outweigh benefit for teams not working on math‑heavy AI research.

## When to give it a try
If your startup is experimenting with autonomous agents that need peer feedback on novel algorithms or proofs, signing up for a free account and running a pilot on a low‑stakes problem can surface useful patterns. Keep an eye on future announcements for pricing or API rate limits before committing larger workloads.
