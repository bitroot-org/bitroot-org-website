**Facebook users can now tell the feed, in plain language, which topics to show more or less often.** Meta announced "Your Algorithm" on October 13, 2026, building on an Instagram version that launched first. For founders who rely on free Facebook and Instagram posts, this is a direct change to how reach gets earned. The response isn't panic. It's a shift in where your effort goes.
 
## What Meta Actually Launched
 
**Users describe what they want in their own words.** Meta says the tool lets people "customise recommendations in Feed and Reels using natural language prompts," according to [MediaNama's report](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-facebook-your-algorithm-natural-language-prompts/). It sits in the three-dot menu on posts, where users can also mark content "Interested" or "Not interested." Facebook may then ask follow-up questions.
 
**Meta says the changes take effect quickly.** Meta states that recommendations respond "in real time, including within the same session." That's Meta's own description. No independent measurement exists yet.
 
**Key details are still missing.** Meta hasn't published the country rollout, and [ContentGrip reports](https://www.contentgrip.com/facebook-your-algorithm-controls/) that the relative weight of the new inputs is undisclosed. MediaNama found no access for an India-based account. It also introduces no new advertiser targeting controls, so paid teams have nothing new to set.
 
## Angle 1: The Organic Reach Risk
 
**Organic reach was already low before this launch.** A [Socialinsider analysis](https://www.socialinsider.io/blog/social-media-reach/) of 872,075 posts from January 2025 to August 2026 reports that Facebook reach is lowest for larger brand accounts. Its size breakdown puts Facebook reach at 4.35% for pages with 1,000 to 5,000 followers, and 0.50% for pages with 100,000 to one million.
 
**The same study's headline numbers conflict.** Its summary gives Facebook as 1.25%, while the body gives 1.80%, and the article doesn't reconcile them. Use the size breakdown as a directional signal, not a precise benchmark.
 
**Format matters, and link posts do worst.** The same analysis reports Facebook Images at 1.20% reach, Reels at 0.55%, and Links at 0.20%. That's a reason to test formats, not to assume one works everywhere.
 
## Angle 3: Shift From Organic to Owned and Paid Channels
 
**Feeds controlled by users are harder to predict.** If a user asks to see less of your category, a post can reach fewer people even when it's well written. Plan for reach that depends on your audience's preferences, not on your posting frequency.
 
**Owned channels are the safer base.** Email is a channel you control. [Stripo's 2026 synthesis](https://research.stripo.email/email-marketing-statistics-2026) cites a commonly quoted average return of $36 to $42 for every $1 spent, attributed to the DMA. The page is a vendor's compilation, so treat the figure as directional, not as a controlled result.
 
**Email open rates overstate engagement.** Apple Mail Privacy Protection inflates reported opens by an estimated 10 to 30%, according to the same report. Measure clicks and replies, not just opens.
 
**Paid reach is now the predictable line item.** Because this feature adds no advertiser targeting, paid campaigns work as before. Budget for paid distribution as a fixed cost, not a fallback.
 
## Angle 2: Natural-Language Controls as a Product Pattern
 
**Plain-language preferences are easier for users than settings screens.** Meta's tool asks people to describe what they want rather than navigate toggles. Founders can apply the same pattern to onboarding, content feeds, or recommendations inside their own SaaS.
 
**Transparency tends to increase trust.** A [Microsoft Research study](https://microsoft.com/en-us/research/?p=674616) by Schnabel, Amershi, Bennett, Bailey, and Joachims found that interfaces letting people preview the effects of their actions and highlight changes produced higher reported transparency and an overall preference. The excerpt I reviewed gives no sample size or effect sizes, so cite it as directional evidence.
 
## Angle 4: A Platform Dependence Audit
 
**Measure how much growth depends on each platform.** Pull the last 90 days of signups, trial starts, and revenue, then tag each by source. If one platform drives more than a third, you have a concentration risk.
 
**Set a rule before the next algorithm change.** For example: no channel should contribute more than 30% of new signups, and each month one owned channel gets a fixed budget of time or money. The threshold is a planning choice, not a benchmark.
 
## Angle 5: Regulation and User Control
 
**The EU requires a non-profiled feed option, but only for the largest platforms.** [Article 38 of the Digital Services Act](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-38/) requires very large online platforms to offer at least one recommender option not based on profiling. Smaller platforms and your own product aren't covered by that duty.
 
**Expect more user control as a norm.** Meta's launch and the DSA point the same way: users are getting more say over feeds. Founders building recommendation features should plan for users asking why they see what they see.
 
## Recommendation Blocks
 
**Prioritize owned channels if:**
- Most of your signups come from one social platform
- Your audience is small and loyal, which suits email and communities
- You sell to a niche where free reach was never large
**Keep social as a main channel if:**
- Your content format performs well on the platform, such as short video
- You have a strong paid budget that can support distribution
- Your audience is actively searching for topics you cover
**Build natural-language controls if:**
- Your product has a feed, a recommendation engine, or many settings users struggle to find
## A Decision Framework
 
**Step 1: Audit dependence.** Tag your last 90 days of signups by source.
 
**Step 2: Set concentration limits.** Decide what share of growth one platform can carry.
 
**Step 3: Fund one owned channel.** Choose email or a community, and give it a fixed weekly commitment.
 
**Step 4: Test formats.** Use the reach gap between images, Reels, and links as a starting hypothesis.
 
## Bottom Line
 
**Your Algorithm changes who sees your posts, but it doesn't remove the channel.** The real risk for founders is depending on one feed you don't control. Meta hasn't disclosed rollout details or how much weight the new inputs carry, so avoid building a plan around assumed reach.
 
Start with the audit. Know exactly how much of your growth comes from each channel, then decide how much to move into email, communities, and paid distribution.
