At its annual Roblox Developer Conference (September 11, 2026), the company made a strategic bet that could either save or cannibalize itself: Roblox is making it easier to build games with AI *and letting creators publish them outside Roblox entirely*.
 
The announcement has three parts:
 
**Part 1: Build Expands Globally**
 
Roblox's "Build" feature — the AI game creation tool launched in July — is expanding beyond New Zealand to Serbia and Singapore. Desktop access is now available. You type a natural language prompt ("cozy adventure game set in a forest"), and AI generates gameplay mechanics, environments, characters, visuals, and sound. Then you iterate and refine. No coding required.
 
**Part 2: Standalone Apps (The Big Move)**
 
Here's what changes the game: Creators can now deploy their Roblox games as *standalone applications* across PC, consoles, mobile, and web. Players don't need a Roblox account. Players don't need the Roblox app. They can play directly from the browser (by end of 2026) or download as a standalone app.
 
**Part 3: Offline Play Coming**
 
By mid-2027, creators can offer offline solo play alongside live multiplayer modes.
 
## Why This Is Radical
 
For 20 years, Roblox built a walled garden. Players lived inside the Roblox app, discovered games through the Roblox store, and earned virtual currency within the Roblox ecosystem. The platform took its cut.
 
Now Roblox is saying: "Make games on our platform using our AI tools. Then take them wherever you want."
 
That's a bet on *reach over control*. The company is willing to lose some platform lock-in if it means Roblox games get distributed to a vastly larger addressable market. A game built in Roblox but played on consoles, mobile app stores, and the web reaches orders of magnitude more players than a game that only lives inside the Roblox app.
 
## The Reality Check
 
"No-code game creation" is only half-true. Build generates an initial prototype, but successful games require iteration, balancing, creative refinement. It's faster than traditional game dev, not frictionless.
 
Also notice: Roblox developers have earned over $5 billion since 2013 ($1.7B in the last year alone). The platform is protecting creator economics while opening distribution. That's strategic — it keeps creators incentivized while expanding the pie.
 
## What This Means for Indie Developers
 
The Google autocomplete image you provided shows real search demand around "game dev with roblox" — the query traffic proves there's a market of people curious about building on Roblox.
 
For indie game devs, this is worth testing:
 
**The upside:** AI-assisted creation speeds up prototyping. Multi-platform distribution means one game reaches console players, mobile players, and browser players without separate ports. Revenue sharing with Roblox.
 
**The downside:** You're competing with every other AI-generated game on the platform. Discovery is algorithmically driven based on retention. The "something will play my game" feeling is gone if it doesn't retain players.
 
## The Bottom Line
 
Roblox is betting that by democratizing game creation (AI) and distribution (standalone apps), they lose some control but gain massive market expansion. That's a classic platform play: become infrastructure, not just a destination.
 
For founders and indie developers, the question isn't "should I build on Roblox?" It's "what's my fastest path to a playable game + multi-platform distribution?" If that answer is "AI-assisted Roblox creation," then the pieces just lined up.
 
The walled garden just opened its gates. Watch what flows out.
