Rely, an indie automation puzzle, opened its browser beta this week. The web version runs in any modern browser, supports keyboard & mouse, and includes a guided tutorial plus three beta scenarios that progressively introduce unreliable components like Sorters and Marker‑Gates.

## What the game asks you to do
The core loop mirrors classic factory builders: you place conveyors, sorters, counters, and markers, then watch components randomly fail. Your objective is to deliver three 100 % reliable items in a row. The game forces you to think in terms of redundancy, inspection, and error handling—skills familiar to reliability engineers.

## How it’s packaged
Rely is hosted at its public page [think‑twice.me/public/rely](https://think-twice.me/public/rely/), built with the Kit framework. The site offers a "Play" button that launches the prototype, and a sign‑up form for development updates. No download is required; everything runs client‑side.

## Limits you should know
The beta only contains three scenarios, and the full game is still under development. The page does not list any pricing, subscription model, or roadmap beyond “more complex components and new challenges.” Because it’s a prototype, performance may vary across browsers, and there’s no guarantee of feature completeness.

## Why it might matter to engineers
Even though it’s a game, Rely models reliability concepts in an interactive way. Engineers can experiment with redundancy patterns without writing code, and the visual feedback can inspire design discussions about fault tolerance in real systems.

**When to try it** – If you’re looking for a low‑commit, free way to spark conversation about reliability engineering in your team, spin up the beta during a lunch‑and‑learn and see how quickly the puzzles expose hidden assumptions.
