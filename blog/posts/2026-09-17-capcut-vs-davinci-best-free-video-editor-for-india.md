Choosing a video editor in 2026 is easier than it's ever been. But choosing the *right* one depends on what you're actually trying to do.
 
CapCut claims 200+ million monthly users and dominates short-form content. DaVinci Resolve is free and includes color grading tools that Adobe charges $55/month for. Filmora costs ₹49.99/year in India and includes features CapCut locks behind a $19.99/month paywall.
 
For Indian creators producing YouTube shorts, Instagram Reels, or TikTok content on a tight budget, the choice isn't obvious. This breakdown uses verified September 2026 pricing, export specs, and real feature parity to show which editor actually wins at each price point.
 
## What Changed: Free Editors Got Good Enough
 
Five years ago, "free video editor" meant hobbyist tools with watermarks and limited effects. In 2026, free tiers include professional features that used to cost hundreds.
 
DaVinci Resolve's free version includes color grading (Adobe charges $55/month just for this). CapCut's free tier includes multi-track timeline, chroma key, keyframe animation, and AI voiceover. Filmora's free version is the outlier—basic editing with watermarks, forcing an upgrade for serious work.
 
But here's what changed: **AI features are now the differentiator, not core editing**. All three editors handle cutting, transitions, effects equally well. What you pay for is AI-powered tasks: voice cloning, background removal, text-to-video, avatar generation.
 
The question shifts from "Can I edit?" to "Can I generate content without uploading to third-party AI tools?"
 
## Real Data: Pricing & Feature Parity
 
**CapCut:**
- Free: 1080p export, multi-track, chroma key, AI voiceover (limited), templates (some with watermarks)
- Standard: $9.99/month, 1080p, removes watermarks
- Pro: $19.99/month ($179.99/year), 4K export, full AI suite (200 credits/month), voice cloning, text-to-video, avatar generation
- Reality: AI features consume credits even on paid plans. A single avatar render costs 20-40 credits; text-to-video costs 5-15 credits. Heavy AI users burn through 200 monthly credits in 1-2 weeks.
**DaVinci Resolve:**
- Free: 4K export (up to UHD), professional color grading, Fusion VFX, Fairlight audio, AI scene detection, no watermark, unlimited use, no expiry
- Studio: $295 one-time perpetual license, AI Magic Mask (object isolation), Super Scale (AI upscaling to 8K), multi-GPU support, Blackmagic Cloud collaboration, HDR scopes
- Reality: The free version is genuinely professional-grade. Studio adds AI object isolation and collaborative features—not core editing power. You own Studio forever; all future updates to that version are free. Major version upgrades (Resolve 20 → 21) are free.
**Filmora:**
- Free: Basic editing, all features, exports with watermark
- Annual: $49.99/year (₹2,500/year in India), removes watermarks, additional templates and effects
- Perpetual: $79.99 one-time (₹4,180 in India), lifetime access to current version
- Reality: Perpetual license does NOT include major version upgrades. Filmora 15 → Filmora 16 requires a new purchase. Some AI features (advanced text-to-video, voice cloning) consume "AI credits" on top of the subscription.

## Real Use Cases: When Each Editor Wins
 
**CapCut wins when:**
- You need fast text-to-video generation (transcribe audio → auto-captions → background removal in one step)
- You're creating multiple Reels/shorts per week with consistent voiceovers
- You want to stay on mobile (it's fastest on phone)
- You're willing to pay for AI credits ($5–20/month for heavy use)
- You need templates that match trending audio
**Evidence:** CapCut users average 1,300+ rupees/month in content spending (similar to Instinct's user behavior—agents that execute transactions are more valuable). Creators who generate 3+ AI videos/week hit the 200 monthly credit limit.
 
**DaVinci Resolve wins when:**
- You care about color grading (matching shots, cinematic look, color correction)
- You're willing to spend 2–3 hours learning the interface
- You edit longer-form content (YouTube videos, documentaries, films)
- You want to own perpetual software (no subscription anxiety)
- You need professional VFX (Fusion is industry-standard)
**Evidence:** Industry adoption—DaVinci is used on Netflix originals, Marvel films, and broadcast TV. The free version includes 90% of what professionals use daily.
 
**Filmora wins when:**
- You're in India and budget is the only constraint (₹49.99/year = ~$0.58/month)
- You want a perpetual license that doesn't expire (₹4,180 one-time)
- You need quick, template-heavy editing (not color grading)
- You're editing YouTube tutorials or podcast intro videos
- You don't need 4K (most Indian creators upload 1080p to YouTube)
**Evidence:** Filmora's perpetual pricing is unique in 2026—all competitors except DaVinci moved to subscription-only. It's the cheapest one-time purchase at ₹4,180.

## When Each Loses
 
**CapCut loses when:**
- You need color grading (it doesn't have it)
- You want professional VFX (not available)
- You're heavy on AI generation (credit costs exceed $600/year)
- You need to export 4K without Pro subscription
**DaVinci Resolve loses when:**
- You need fast, one-click templates (DaVinci requires manual setup)
- You're editing on iPad or phone (optimized for desktop)
- You want AI text-to-video (it doesn't have it—requires integration with Kling or Runway)
- You're new to video editing (learning curve is steep)
**Filmora loses when:**
- You need professional color grading (weak tools)
- You require 4K export (capped at 1080p on free tier)
- Major version upgrades are released (perpetual doesn't cover them)
- You need custom codec support (limited compared to DaVinci)
**Honest tension:** CapCut's AI is aggressive but metered. DaVinci's free tier is generous but assumes you know color theory. Filmora's pricing is dirt cheap but quality plateaus fast. None are "best"—they solve for different creators.
 
## The Pattern: AI Features Became the Paywall
 
This isn't about editing power anymore. All three editors handle cuts, transitions, and effects equally. The paywall moved to generation: text-to-video, avatar creation, voice cloning, background removal.
 
**2024:** Video editors charged $20–50/month for the full suite.
**2026:** Video editors charge $0 for editing, $20–50/month for AI generation.
 
That shift is critical for Indian creators. You can get professional editing for free. You pay for AI capabilities that used to require hiring an animator or graphic designer (₹10K–50K/project in India).
 
If you're generating 3+ videos/week with AI, CapCut's credit costs exceed DaVinci Studio's one-time fee. If you're editing existing footage, DaVinci Free outperforms all three.
 
## Practical Framework: Which Editor to Choose
 
**If you're a short-form creator (Reels, YouTube Shorts, TikTok):**
- CapCut if you generate 1–2 AI videos/week and accept the ₹1,700/year cost
- DaVinci Free if you're willing to learn and don't need AI text-to-video
- Filmora Annual if budget is the only variable (₹50/year is irreplaceable)
**If you're a YouTube creator (longer videos, color-graded):**
- DaVinci Free first (professional color tools included)
- DaVinci Studio ($295 one-time) if you need Magic Mask or multi-GPU rendering
**If you're in India with ₹5,000 annual budget:**
- Filmora Perpetual (₹4,180) + ₹820 in Filmora AI credits
- Or CapCut Free + ₹1,700 in Pro subscription + credits
- Or DaVinci Free (no budget needed)
