Google announced Gemini Omni for Google Ads on August 25, 2026. The pitch: Free AI video generation directly in Asset Studio. Create, edit, and scale on-brand videos with a text prompt. No extra cost.
 
It sounds generous. It's not. It's a lock-in strategy.
 
---
 
## Why Google Gives Away Gemini Omni
 
The announcement included a statistic: "Over half of SMBs globally on Google Ads now use Google AI to create or optimize their creative."
 
That's not a success metric for SMBs. That's a lock-in metric for Google.
 
Here's the economics: If 50%+ of SMBs depend on Google's free AI tools, they're 50%+ dependent on Google's ecosystem. Switching away from Google Ads means abandoning those workflows, recreating them elsewhere, retraining on new tools.
 
Free tools drive adoption. Adoption drives dependency. Dependency creates switching costs. Switching costs enable price increases.
 
This is how platforms scale lock-in.
 
---
 
## How Gemini Omni Creates Lock-In (Step by Step)
 
**Step 1: Data Embedding**
 
The workflow starts with uploading. Users import "existing brand guidelines and URL into Asset Studio." Your brand guidelines are now inside Google's system. The AI model trains on your specific visual and tone standards, learning your brand DNA.
 
That data lives in Google's infrastructure. Leaving means abandoning all that context training.
 
**Step 2: Workflow Integration**
 
Users generate videos directly inside Google Ads. They export "multi-format assets directly into your Demand Gen, Performance Max, or other YouTube or Google campaigns."
 
The workflow is now: Brief → Generate in Google → Deploy in Google. Every step happens inside Google's ecosystem.
 
Switching means rebuilding this entire workflow elsewhere. Different UI, different integration points, different processes.
 
**Step 3: Adoption Scaling**
 
Once SMBs use Gemini Omni for a few campaigns, it becomes routine. The team learns the workflow. New team members are trained on it. Creative briefs are written for it. It's not a tool anymore—it's a process.
 
Switching costs escalate with adoption because it's not just learning new software. It's retraining the team, rewriting processes, rebuilding workflows.
 
---
 
## The Economic Reality
 
Gemini Omni is free. But the cost of leaving Google Ads isn't zero.
 
**What switching actually costs:**
- Time to recreate workflows in competitor platforms (Shopify, TikTok, Facebook)
- Team retraining on new tools
- Rebuilding creative briefs and processes
- Data migration complexity
- Lost productivity during transition
This is the hidden cost of "free." It's not the tool that costs money. It's leaving that costs money.
 
---
 
## Why Google Does This
 
Platform companies use free tools to accomplish one goal: increase switching costs.
 
Here's the incentive structure:
 
**For Google:** Every SMB using Gemini Omni is an SMB that's harder to leave. They'll tolerate higher costs from Google's search ads, higher CPC rates, or new features bundled into "premium" tiers. They're sunk into Google's ecosystem.
 
**For SMBs:** They save money on creative production tools today. But they pay that back later through higher ad costs, reduced negotiating power, and inability to diversify platforms.
 
The math is simple: Google gives away $500 in free AI video generation to lock users into platforms where they'll spend $50,000 on ads.
 
---
 
## The Pattern Google Didn't Invent
 
This isn't new strategy. Amazon does it with free Prime shipping (locks users into marketplace). Microsoft does it with free OneDrive (locks users into Office 365 ecosystem). Stripe does it with free webhooks and basic integrations (locks developers into Stripe).
 
The playbook: Make the tool generous enough to drive rapid adoption. Embed it into workflows. Increase switching costs through dependency. Then extract value through ecosystem lock-in.
 
Google is applying it to SMB marketing.
 
---
 
## What Founders Should Learn
 
Two lessons here:
 
**First:** If you're using free tools deeply integrated into your workflow, calculate the switching cost. Map your data. Understand the workflows. Know the cost of leaving. If it's high, you've lost negotiating power.
 
**Second:** If you're building SaaS, this is the playbook to consider. Free initial tools create adoption. Locked-in workflows create retention. Retention creates pricing power.
 
The goal isn't for the free tool to be profitable. The goal is for the ecosystem to be profitable.
 
---
 
## The Real Cost of Convenience
 
Gemini Omni saves SMBs time and money on creative production. That's real value. But the value of convenience is often paid for in lock-in costs later.
 
When Google inevitably increases CPC costs, bundles Gemini Omni into premium tiers, or limits free API access, SMBs will face a choice: pay the new price or rebuild everything elsewhere.
 
Most will pay the new price.
 
That's the math Google is betting on. That's why Gemini Omni is free.
 
---
 
## The Decision Framework
 
Before adopting deeply integrated free tools, ask:
 
1. **Switching cost:** How expensive would it be to move to a competitor?
2. **Dependency depth:** How critical is this tool to my workflow?
3. **Lock-in timeline:** When will the free tier end or get restricted?
4. **Negotiating power:** If costs increase, can I leave?
If switching costs are high, dependency is deep, and you have no negotiating power, you're not using a free tool. You're entering a lock-in arrangement.
 
Gemini Omni is good. But it's not free. It's paid for in platform dependency.
